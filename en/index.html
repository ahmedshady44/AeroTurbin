Why common scrubbers fail here

Common scrubbers

  • They draw a lot of electricity.
  • Their sorbents stop working properly above 60 °C. Heat makes captured gas escape.

AeroTurbine's approach

  • It makes its own power from the exhaust. Design target: up to 122.6 W.
  • Cooling fins bring the gas below 60 °C before it reaches the filter cartridge. This is the design intent.

Show the formulas
  1. Turbine power = 50.6 W × flow^1.5, where flow is 0 to 1.
  2. TEG power = 72.0 W × tempFactor × flow. tempFactor = (exhaust temperature − 100) ÷ 300, kept between 0 and 1.
  3. Total power = turbine + TEG. The most it can reach is 50.6 + 72.0 = 122.6 W.
  4. Daily energy (Wh) = total power (W) × operating hours.
  5. Pollutant reduction = 70% × filterHealth.
  6. filterHealth = (1 − 0.6 × load²) × heatFactor. Load is operating hours since the swap divided by 600 h, kept between 0 and 1. So without heat, health falls from 1 to 0.4 at saturation.
  7. heatFactor = 1 when the cartridge gas is 60 °C or less. Above that it drops by 0.6 for every 40 °C, to a minimum of 0.4.

Demo assumptions, not design facts: the 100 to 400 °C hot-side range, the 600 h cartridge life and the heat penalty. They only make the demo move. Replace them with measured values once the prototype is tested.

The circular loop Concept

  1. 1Communities swap cartridges through a subscription.
  2. 2Spent cartridges go to municipal recycling centers.
  3. 3By-products (industrial gypsum, CaSO4) are sold to cement and wallboard makers.
  4. 4The revenue helps buy fresh sorbent for new cartridges.
